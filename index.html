British Indian Reform Acts (19th Century)

Key legislative enactments resulting from reform movements

Sati Abolition Regulation (Regulation XVII of 1829) 1829

Passed by Governor-General Lord William Bentinck in Bengal. Championed by Raja Ram Mohan Roy, it declared the burning or burying alive of Hindu widows illegal and punishable as culpable homicide.

Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act (Act XV of 1856) 1856

Enacted by Lord Canning (drafted under Lord Dalhousie). Championed by Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar, it legally validated widow remarriage and declared children born of such marriages legitimate.

Female Infanticide Prevention Act (Act VIII of 1870) 1870

Passed to curb the killing of female newborns in Northwestern Provinces and Punjab. Strongly advocated by social reformers and Sikh leaders to protect girl infants.

Native Marriage Act / Civil Marriage Act (Act III of 1872) 1872

Advocated by Keshab Chandra Sen. Prohibited polygamy, permitted inter-caste marriages, and set the minimum marriage age at 14 for girls and 18 for boys.

Age of Consent Act (Act X of 1891) 1891

Championed by Parsi reformer Behramji Malabari. Raised the age of sexual consent for girls from 10 to 12 years across British India to protect child brides from marital violence.
